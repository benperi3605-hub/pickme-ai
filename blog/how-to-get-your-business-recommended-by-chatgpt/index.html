How to get your business recommended by ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude

I run PickMe AI, an AI visibility agency in Boston. Every week I ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and the other AI tools the same questions a customer would ask, and I record who they name. Most small businesses never come up, and the reason is almost never the quality of the business. It is that the AI has nothing clear to read about them.

Here is what moves the needle, in plain words.

AI assistants name two or three businesses, not ten

When someone asks "who's the best auto body shop in South Boston?" the answer is a short list. To be on it, the assistant has to know three things with confidence: what you do, where you do it, and why someone should pick you. If any of those is fuzzy on your website, you are easy to skip.

The platforms that matter

The ones we cover and measure: ChatGPT, Claude, Google Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Meta AI, Grok, Perplexity and Muse, and we add new ones as they launch. They read the web differently, but they all reward the same thing: plain, consistent, verifiable facts.

What we actually change on a website

  • Schema markup. A label layer behind the page that tells AI tools, in their own format, who you are, what you offer, your hours, your service area and how to reach you. Every fact in it also appears in the visible text, so nothing is hidden or made up.
  • An llms.txt file. A short plain-text summary of the business at yoursite.com/llms.txt, written for machines that read text, not design.
  • An FAQ written from your facts. The questions people actually ask, answered in one or two sentences each, so an assistant can quote you directly.
  • Business attributes. The specific things customers ask about in your category (free estimates, works with insurance, open weekends) stated once, clearly, where they belong.
  • Listings and off-site mentions. Google, Bing, Apple and Yelp saying the same name, phone and hours as your site; plus the Reddit threads, blog posts and backlinks AI tools scan when they decide who to trust.

Then we keep updating all of it, because what customers ask shifts every week.

How you know it worked

We ask the AI tools real customer questions from your category, record whether they name you, and report one AI visibility score every month: the percentage of questions where your business showed up. We also track the customers AI tools refer to you and the revenue those referrals turn into, and share both monthly. One week is a snapshot; a trend takes three.

Where to start

Look at your own site the way an assistant would: can a stranger tell, in ten seconds, what you do, where, and why you? If not, that is the first fix.
